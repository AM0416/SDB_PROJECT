## Abstract

This paper documents observations derived from Real-Time Narrative Records (RTNRs) and examines how subtle characteristics of language may influence interpretation during human–AI interaction.

Rather than proposing a universal theory, the paper presents a structured account of observed phenomena and the research artifacts generated from those observations.

The concepts of "temperature" and "humidity" are introduced as descriptive metaphors for aspects of communication that were repeatedly observed but were not adequately represented by conventional linguistic categories alone.

# 1. Introduction

Human communication contains information beyond literal semantics.

Throughout the observations documented in this project, repeated situations emerged in which participants perceived differences in language that could not be sufficiently described using existing lexical or grammatical categories alone.

To describe these observations, the metaphors of "temperature" and "humidity" are adopted.

These metaphors are not presented as objective scientific variables.

They are observational concepts introduced to describe recurring characteristics found within the recorded narratives.
