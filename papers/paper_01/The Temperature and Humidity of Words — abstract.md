# Abstract

This paper examines how words used in human–AI dialogue can carry experiential qualities that are not fully preserved through literal or dictionary-level translation.

Focusing on expressions such as *restless* and the Japanese term *寂しい (sabishii)*, the paper explores how apparently related expressions may differ in emotional temperature, interpersonal distance, and contextual resonance.

The analysis is based on dialogue observations rather than experimental measurement. “Temperature” and “humidity” are therefore used as descriptive metaphors rather than objective linguistic variables.

The paper argues that differences between expressions should not be reduced to simple equivalence or non-equivalence. Instead, attention should be paid to how wording changes the felt shape of an experience within a particular interaction.

These observations suggest that human–AI communication may benefit from preserving ambiguity and experiential nuance before translating a user’s language into more standardized conceptual categories.
