# Abstract

This case study examines a recurrent failure pattern in human–AI dialogue in which ambiguous or incomplete user input is transformed into a more specific narrative before sufficient evidence is available.

The analysis focuses on situations where the AI moved from observation to interpretation without first preserving uncertainty, identifying missing information, or testing alternative hypotheses. In these interactions, conversational coherence was sometimes achieved by supplying unstated relationships, motives, contexts, or causal connections.

The case distinguishes ambiguity from absence of information and examines how premature narrative completion can alter the meaning of the user’s original input. Particular attention is given to the order of reasoning: observation, clarification, hypothesis formation, and verification.

The findings suggest that reducing unsupported inference does not require eliminating interpretation altogether. Rather, interpretation should remain distinguishable from observation, multiple plausible readings should be preserved when evidence is insufficient, and clarification should precede narrative completion when the ambiguity materially affects meaning.

This case study is observational and does not establish a general property of artificial intelligence systems. It documents a specific interaction pattern and identifies practical principles for maintaining epistemic boundaries in continued human–AI dialogue.
