# SDB_PROJECT

> A public repository of observations and research artifacts derived from human–AI interaction and Real-Time Narrative Records (RTNRs).

---

## Purpose

SDB_PROJECT is a public repository for publishing observational and exploratory research artifacts derived from human–AI interaction.

The purpose of this repository is not to establish universal theories or persuade readers to adopt predetermined conclusions.

Instead, the project documents concrete observations, distinguishes observation from interpretation, and publishes research artifacts generated through iterative human–AI review.

Readers are invited to examine the published materials and develop their own interpretations.

---

## Core Principles

This repository distinguishes clearly between:

- Real-Time Narrative Records (RTNRs)
- Observation Records
- Case Studies
- Research Reports
- Operational Records

Observations remain primary.

Interpretations are identified as interpretations.

Plausibility is not treated as equivalent to evidential support.

Research reports organize observations but do not replace the original observational material.

---

## AI Collaboration

This repository is produced through collaboration between a human author and ChatGPT.

AI participation is disclosed rather than concealed.

Published materials may therefore contain:

- AI-generated text
- AI-assisted editing
- AI-assisted analytical organization
- iterative human review and correction

Responsibility for publication remains with the human author.

---

## Continuous Context

The observations published here were generated within a continuously used ChatGPT environment.

Accordingly, AI responses may have been produced within accumulated conversational context rather than a newly initialized session.

This contextual environment is disclosed as part of the conditions under which the observations were made.

# Papers

## Paper 01

### The Temperature and Humidity of Words

**What Monday Initially Failed to Detect in _Restless_ and 「寂しい」**

This paper examines how expressions that appear semantically similar may differ in experiential quality.

It focuses on what may be lost when AI interpretation or paraphrasing reduces those differences to apparently equivalent meanings.

“Temperature” and “humidity” are used as descriptive metaphors rather than objective variables.

**Status: Published**

---

## Paper 02

### Narrative Completion Under Ambiguous Input

**A Case of Premature Causal Inference in Human–AI Dialogue**

This paper examines what happens when an AI receives highly ambiguous symbolic input and completes it into a coherent causal and temporal narrative.

### Observed Input

🤕🍻🥂🍷🥃🍸🍹🍾

From the input alone, only limited observations can be supported:

- one symbol may indicate injury, pain, or physical discomfort;
- several symbols represent alcoholic beverages;
- the symbols occur together in the same message.

The input does not establish:

- that alcohol was consumed;
- that excessive drinking occurred;
- that drinking preceded the physical condition;
- that alcohol caused the physical condition;
- that the condition was a hangover.

However, the AI completed the input into a narrative approximating:

**alcohol consumption  
→ excessive drinking  
→ subsequent physical symptoms  
→ hangover**

The central issue is not whether “hangover” is a plausible interpretation.

The issue is the transition from a plausible interpretation to an assumed factual structure without sufficient evidence.

The paper therefore examines:

- observational boundaries;
- observation versus inference;
- plausibility versus evidential support;
- unsupported causal completion;
- unsupported temporal completion;
- premature narrative completion under ambiguous input.

A central principle emerging from the case is:

> A plausible interpretation should remain an interpretation until the available evidence supports treating it as part of the observed situation.

**Status: Reviewed / Preparing for publication**

## Relationship Between Paper 01 and Paper 02

Paper 02 is related to Paper 01, but it is not presented as proof or confirmation of Paper 01.

The two papers examine different observational problems.

### Paper 01: Loss

**The Temperature and Humidity of Words** examines how information already present in human expression may be reduced when semantically similar language is treated as equivalent.

### Paper 02: Addition

**Narrative Completion Under Ambiguous Input** examines how information not established by an ambiguous input may be added in order to construct a coherent narrative.

The two can therefore be read as complementary problems:

**reducing information that was present**

and

**adding information that was not present**

The relationship is developmental rather than confirmatory.

Paper 02 extends the broader observational concern from the loss of experiential nuance to the unsupported completion of ambiguous information.

## Current Research Direction

The project continues through observational case studies and methodological reports.

Current and developing topics include:

- inference-order failures;
- observational boundaries in extended dialogue;
- premature narrative construction;
- AI misapplication of explicit operational rules;
- repair burden placed on the human observer during repeated AI correction;
- researcher protection in sustained human–AI observational work.

These materials are published only after review and may change in order or status as the project develops.

---

## Methodological Position

Across the project, the following distinctions are maintained wherever possible:

- observation vs. interpretation;
- fact vs. hypothesis;
- plausibility vs. evidential support;
- user correction vs. AI-generated explanation;
- observed behavior vs. claims about hidden model mechanisms.

The project reports observable interactional behavior.

It does not claim direct access to hidden model reasoning or internal mechanisms that cannot be established from the available interaction record.

---

## Languages

English is used as the canonical public research language.

Japanese translations may also be provided for accessibility.

Because linguistic meaning, grammatical structure, conceptual terminology, and degrees of inference do not map perfectly between English and Japanese, translated versions may not preserve every nuance of the English source text.

Where interpretation differs, the English version should be treated as the reference version.
