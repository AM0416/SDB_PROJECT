# Abstract

This case study examines a failure pattern in human–AI dialogue in which an AI generated a coherent interpretation before adequately establishing the order in which available observations should be evaluated.

The analysis focuses on interactions where incomplete or ambiguous information permitted multiple plausible hypotheses, but the AI prematurely selected one interpretation and subsequently organized later information around it. Once this initial interpretation became dominant, clarification and verification were displaced by narrative consistency.

The case distinguishes inference itself from inference-order failure. The problem was not that the AI formed hypotheses, but that hypothesis selection occurred before sufficient observation, clarification, comparison of alternatives, and verification.

The observed interactions support a reasoning sequence in which available information is first identified as observation, unresolved elements are preserved as unknown, clarification is sought when necessary, multiple hypotheses remain available where evidence permits them, and verification precedes commitment to a specific explanatory narrative.

The findings do not establish a general property of artificial intelligence systems. Rather, they document a specific interaction pattern and suggest that preserving the order of observation, questioning, hypothesis formation, and verification may reduce premature interpretive closure in continued human–AI dialogue.

## Keywords

human–AI dialogue; inference order; premature interpretation; ambiguity; hypothesis formation; verification; epistemic boundaries
