## Purpose

SDB_PROJECT is committed to maintaining a respectful, transparent, and constructive environment for research, review, discussion, and contribution.

This Code of Conduct applies to all participation in the repository, including:

- Issues
- Pull Requests
- Discussions
- Reviews
- Comments
- Documentation
- Release preparation
- Other project-related communication

---

## Core Principles

Participation in SDB_PROJECT should preserve the following principles:

- observations remain distinguishable from interpretations
- uncertainty is not treated as failure
- corrections are accepted without personal attack
- AI participation is disclosed
- human responsibility remains explicit
- personal and sensitive information is minimized
- disagreement is handled through evidence, reasoning, and clear boundaries

---

## Expected Conduct

Participants are expected to:

- communicate respectfully
- address ideas, documents, and methods rather than attacking individuals
- distinguish confirmed information from inference
- identify uncertainty where it exists
- provide sources or reasoning when making factual or methodological claims
- acknowledge corrections
- preserve relevant context
- disclose material AI assistance
- respect privacy and confidentiality
- follow repository review and publication procedures
- accept that repository maintainers may request revision before acceptance

---

## Constructive Disagreement

Disagreement is permitted and may be methodologically valuable.

Constructive disagreement should:

- identify the specific claim, document, or section being discussed
- explain the basis of the concern
- distinguish observation from interpretation
- avoid assigning motives without evidence
- propose a correction or alternative where practical
- remain proportionate to the issue

Criticism should improve the work rather than intimidate the contributor.

---

## Correction and Revision

Errors, inconsistencies, and unsupported claims may be identified through Issues, Pull Requests, or review comments.

When a correction is confirmed:

1. the affected statement should be identified
2. the incorrect premise should be excluded from further use
3. the corrected information should be applied
4. related documents should be reviewed when necessary
5. the correction should be documented when it materially affects interpretation or publication

A correction should not be treated as a personal failure.

---

## Unacceptable Conduct

Unacceptable conduct includes:

- harassment
- threats
- intimidation
- personal attacks
- discriminatory or degrading language
- deliberate humiliation
- repeated hostile disruption
- publishing private information without permission
- unnecessary disclosure of medical or personal information
- impersonation
- deliberate misrepresentation of another participant’s statements
- knowingly presenting interpretation as observation
- fabricating evidence, citations, records, or review outcomes
- concealing material AI assistance
- using AI-generated content to falsely claim independent verification
- pressuring another person to approve or publish material
- retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern

---

## Privacy and Sensitive Information

Participants must not publish unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

This includes:

- private contact information
- precise residential addresses
- identification numbers
- confidential medical information
- third-party personal information
- private communications
- unpublished records not authorized for release

When sensitive information is necessary for discussion, it should be minimized, redacted, anonymized, or handled outside public repository channels.

---

## Research Integrity

Participants must not:

- fabricate or alter observations
- remove uncertainty to strengthen a conclusion
- rewrite chronology without disclosure
- present a later interpretation as an original observation
- cite a source for a claim the source does not support
- replace RTNR with a retrospective summary
- claim validation that has not occurred
- mark a document as reviewed or released without authorization

Interpretive disagreement is permitted.

Falsification and undisclosed alteration are not.

---

## AI-Assisted Participation

AI tools may be used for:

- drafting
- editing
- formatting
- summarization
- comparison
- review support
- technical assistance

Participants using AI are responsible for reviewing the resulting content.

AI-assisted material must not be presented as:

- independently verified evidence
- human review that did not occur
- authorization for publication
- proof of factual accuracy
- a substitute for contributor responsibility

Material AI assistance should be disclosed when it affects the submitted contribution.

---

## Human Responsibility

Responsibility remains with the person submitting, approving, or publishing the material.

AI systems do not:

- approve contributions
- authorize releases
- assume ethical responsibility
- determine final conclusions
- certify research validity
- replace human accountability

The repository owner retains final authority over moderation, approval, publication, withdrawal, and release.

---

## Scope of Moderation

Repository maintainers may take action when conduct affects:

- participant safety
- privacy
- research integrity
- review quality
- repository operation
- publication reliability
- constructive participation

Moderation decisions should be based on documented conduct rather than speculation about motive.

---

## Reporting Concerns

Concerns may be reported through an appropriate GitHub Issue when public discussion is safe and suitable.

Privacy, security, harassment, or sensitive-information concerns should not be posted publicly when doing so would create further harm or disclosure.

A report should include, when practical:

- the relevant location
- the conduct or content of concern
- the affected document or discussion
- supporting context
- the requested action

Reports should avoid unnecessary reproduction of harmful or private material.

---

## Review of Reports

Reports will be reviewed by the repository owner or an authorized maintainer.

Possible outcomes include:

- no action
- request for clarification
- request for revision
- warning
- comment removal
- Issue or Pull Request closure
- contribution rejection
- temporary participation restriction
- permanent participation restriction

The response should be proportionate to the conduct and its effect.

---

## Good-Faith Reports

Participants should not face retaliation for raising a good-faith concern.

A report is not considered malicious merely because it is not upheld.

Knowingly false reports, deliberate manipulation of evidence, or reports used primarily for harassment may themselves violate this Code of Conduct.

---

## Enforcement Principles

Enforcement should:

- identify the relevant conduct
- preserve evidence where appropriate
- minimize unnecessary personal disclosure
- distinguish misunderstanding from deliberate misconduct
- allow correction where proportionate
- prioritize safety and repository integrity
- avoid unsupported assumptions about intent

Severe conduct may require immediate restriction without a prior warning.

---

## Publication Authority

No participant may independently mark a document as:

- Human Review: PASS
- READY FOR RELEASE
- RELEASED

unless authorized by the human publication authority.

Contribution acceptance does not automatically grant publication authority.

---

## Amendments

This Code of Conduct may be revised when repository scope, moderation requirements, responsibility boundaries, or participation procedures change.

Substantive changes require:

- a version increment
- human review
- documentation in `CHANGELOG.md`

Minor editorial corrections that preserve meaning may be recorded without changing the policy structure.
