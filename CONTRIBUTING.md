# Contributing to SDB_PROJECT

**Document ID:** SDB-CONTRIBUTING-001  
**Version:** v1.0.0  
**Status:** READY FOR HUMAN REVIEW

---

## Purpose

This document explains how individuals may contribute to SDB_PROJECT.

Contributions may include:

- correction proposals
- methodological feedback
- editorial review
- terminology review
- documentation improvements
- technical implementation
- figure preparation
- accessibility improvements
- repository maintenance

All contributions must preserve the repository’s core principles:

- observations remain primary
- interpretations remain distinguishable from observations
- AI participation is disclosed
- conclusions remain open
- publication responsibility remains human

---

## Before Contributing

Before opening an Issue or Pull Request, contributors should review:

- `README.md`
- `PROJECT_CHARTER.md`
- `PUBLICATION_POLICY.md`
- `AUTHORS.md`
- `RTNR.md`

Contributions should not contradict the repository’s documented purpose, source hierarchy, or publication policy.

---

## Types of Contributions

### Documentation Corrections

Documentation corrections may include:

- typographical corrections
- broken links
- formatting problems
- inconsistent terminology
- unclear wording
- metadata omissions

Minor editorial corrections should preserve the original meaning.

---

### Methodological Feedback

Methodological feedback may address:

- observation and interpretation separation
- traceability
- source hierarchy
- review procedures
- research limitations
- transparency
- reproducibility
- contextual bias

Methodological feedback should distinguish between:

- identified problems
- interpretations
- recommendations
- alternative approaches

---

### Content Review

Content review may include:

- consistency with RTNR
- consistency between documents
- terminology review
- AI transparency review
- continuous context disclosure
- human responsibility review
- citation review

Review comments should identify the relevant document and section whenever practical.

---

### Technical Contributions

Technical contributions may include:

- repository organization
- Markdown formatting
- automation scripts
- release packaging
- validation tools
- accessibility improvements
- document conversion workflows

Technical changes must not alter the meaning of research documents without explicit review.

---
